Draft for review

If you already have a site plan, mark which wall or structure needs attention. Include the plan’s date and note changes since it was drawn. A house wall, freestanding boundary wall and retaining structure need different questions, so identify the structure’s purpose rather than describing every problem as a crack repair.

Photographs of the wider setting can show how the work relates to buildings, access and visible changes in level. Share earlier reports or repair records and describe changes you have noticed over time. Nearby drainage or vegetation may be relevant context, but neither proves the cause of damage.

Council’s Grose Wold chapter includes site-analysis guidance for a defined development area. This is useful background when gathering existing records, not a rule that every repair customer must commission a survey. Confirm the property-specific requirements before arranging further investigations or new works.

Questions before a quote

Do I need to buy a new survey before asking about a repair?

Start with records you already hold and explain what is missing. Whether further survey, engineering or planning advice is needed depends on the property and proposed work.

Can a plan explain why a wall has cracked?

Not by itself. Plans and photographs help describe the setting and history. They do not replace an assessment of current conditions or identify a cause without investigation.

Should I expose the footing or dig behind a wall first?

No. Do not excavate, remove support or enter unsafe areas to document a problem. Begin with information that can be gathered safely and discuss the appropriate next assessment.
