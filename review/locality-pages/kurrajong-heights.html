Draft for review

An enquiry about damaged brick or stonework is different from a proposal for a new boundary wall. Explain what is already there, which part concerns you and whether you want a repair or a changed design. Send a wider view of the frontage and closer photographs of the affected material, taken from a safe position.

For land within the defined Kurrajong Heights Village area, Council publishes specific character and fencing provisions. Do not assume that a brick replacement is appropriate simply because masonry repairs are needed elsewhere on the property. Check the actual land and proposed work with Council before committing to a new frontage design.

Include earlier approvals, conservation advice or repair records if you have them. Identify sound material you would like retained and any access restrictions. Do not dismantle stonework, remove joints or clear vegetation to prepare for an enquiry.

Questions before a quote

Does the village chapter apply to every Kurrajong Heights address?

No. The chapter identifies a defined area. The current controls and their application to your land and proposal need checking; a suburb name alone does not settle the requirements.

Can an existing fence simply be replaced with brick?

Do not assume so. Describe the existing fence and intended change, then check the applicable planning provisions before choosing materials. A repair enquiry is not approval for a replacement design.

What photographs help?

Show the complete frontage, the affected section and nearby sound joints or masonry. Include dates and earlier repairs if known. Keep clear of unstable structures rather than moving loose material for a better photograph.
